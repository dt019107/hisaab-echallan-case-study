01. The Problem & How We Solved It

Why commercial traffic fines turn into financial chaos — and how Hisaab automates every step

Commercial Fleets & Vehicle Leasing

The Real Problem in Fleets Today

Operational Chaos
  • Police cameras fine the truck, not the driver: When a truck runs a red light or speeds on a highway, the fine is tagged strictly to the vehicle registration number on the government portal (Parivahan). The system does not know who was driving.
  • Drivers change shifts every day: Delivery companies rotate 2 to 3 drivers per vehicle across different shifts. When a ticket arrives 3 weeks later, managers have no easy way to know who had the keys at that exact hour.
  • Lost deadlines mean double fines and locked trucks: Fleet teams track tickets on messy Excel sheets and WhatsApp groups. By the time someone notices, the 15-day court deadline has expired, late fees have doubled, and the government blocks the vehicle from renewing its road permit.

How Hisaab Fixes It (The UX Response)

Engineered Workflow
  • Action-first design, not passive reports: Every screen immediately answers two questions: “Who caused this fine?” and “What is the exact next step?”
  • GPS matching connects fine to driver in seconds: The platform reads the exact minute of the violation and cross-checks the vehicle's GPS trip log and driver roster, instantly showing who was at the wheel with a 98.4% confidence score.
  • Separated Payer vs Responsible Party: The fleet company pays the fine immediately from escrow so the truck stays legal on the road, while the system automatically queues a payroll deduction for the driver or adds it to the rental client's monthly bill.
  • AI helps the user, but humans make the final call: The system automates tedious data entry and rule-matching, but every fine attribution requires a simple one-click human confirmation before money moves.

02. Who Uses Hisaab: The Two Real-World Stories

How the same traffic ticket creates two completely different operational headaches

7 Key Enterprise Stakeholders

In India’s commercial logistics industry, traffic fines affect two primary decision-makers: the Fleet Manager (who runs trucks and manages drivers) and the Lease Operations Lead (who rents vehicles to business clients). Five supporting roles handle payroll deduction, legal court dates, and driver disputes.

1. The Fleet Operator (e.g., Apex Fleet Pvt Ltd)

“We own 842 delivery trucks and employ over 1,200 drivers. When a speeding ticket arrives 3 weeks after the trip, I need to know which employee had the keys at 11:42 AM so we can deduct it from their next paycheck before salary cycle closes.”

Top Priority: Fast driver attribution & automated payroll recovery deduction.
2. The Leasing Enterprise (e.g., Zoom Leasing Co.)

“We lease hundreds of commercial vans to logistics clients like Delhivery. If a client’s driver runs a red light, we must settle the fine immediately so the government doesn’t blacklist our vehicle, then bill the fine back to the client’s monthly invoice with proof.”

Top Priority: Immediate fine clearance & indisputable lease contract audit proof.

07. Core Fleet Objects, Relational Schema & Global IA

To transition ChallanOS from an ad-hoc tracking spreadsheet to an enterprise-grade ERP system, we formalized the domain into 6 core relational entities and established 6 non-negotiable cross-cutting UX principles that govern every table, drawer, and modal.

The 6 Core Relational Fleet Objects

Strict 3NF Schema & Audit Trails
1 : N Entity

Vehicle

Holds the physical commercial asset profile. Has many Challans and many Responsibility Periods across historical fleet cycles.

Keys: reg_number (PK), vin, chassis
Relations: → Challans, → Contracts
Core Object

Challan

A legal citation. Has exactly one Vehicle, one Offence, one Status, one Responsible Party (with explicit business reason), and many Events.

Keys: challan_number (PK), notice_id
Relations: → Vehicle, → Party, → Events
Role Actor

Party

The human or legal actor. Represents Driver (on-duty employee/contractor), Lessee (commercial client), Fleet Operator, or Registered Owner.

Keys: party_id (PK), dl_number, gstin
Relations: → Custody_Periods, → Invoices
Legal Binding

Contract

The commercial leasing agreement. Links Owner, Lessee, Vehicle, effective timestamp windows, and statutory traffic liability clauses (e.g. Clause 7.2).

Keys: lease_id (PK), agreement_ref
Relations: → Vehicles, → Terms_Clause
Gateway Flow

Payment

The financial settlement event. Links Challan, Payer Entity, exact settlement amount, MoRTH gateway reference, receipt PDF, and secondary Recovery Ledger.

Keys: txn_id (PK), morth_ack_ref
Relations: → Challan, → Payroll_Deduction
Judicial Evidence

Dispute

Legal grievance or Virtual Court case. Links Challan, telemetry & ANPR evidence dossiers, statutory appeal deadlines, and court judgment outcome (Waived or Upheld).

Keys: dispute_id (PK), court_case_no
Relations: → Challan, → Evidence_Files

6 Cross-Cutting UX Principles

Universal Product Design Heuristics

1. Status First

One shared status vocabulary and uniform color token mapping used everywhere across cards, table pills, filters, and reports. No conflicting labels.

2. Explain the Why

Every attribution or AI recommendation explicitly renders the deterministic rule, telematics timestamp, or lease contract clause that produced it.

3. Money is Always Visible

Always formatted in Indian Rupee currency standard with tabular figures (number-tabular) and strict right-alignment.

4. Bulk by Default, Detail on Demand

Every data table is equipped with checkbox multi-select and a sticky floating action bar for bulk operations; slide-over drawer reveals deep item context.

5. Never Hide Data Quality

API synchronization timestamp, Parivahan gateway latency, and sync confidence score are treated as first-class UI citizens to preserve user trust.

6. Reversible Actions

Reassignment and dispute appeal pathways are always readily accessible with full cryptographic audit logging and versioned state transitions.

16. AI as a Design Multiplier & Ethical Guardrails

We leveraged modern generative AI as a force multiplier for velocity, never as a replacement for human critical design judgment. Here is the transparent breakdown of where AI accelerated the workflow versus what the designer decided.

Workflow Phase How AI Multiplied Speed What I Decided as Designer
Domain Research
Summarised statutory rules under Motor Vehicles Act, how e-challans propagate to Virtual Courts, lease liability precedents, and compiled a comprehensive enterprise terminology glossary. Verified assumptions against real MoRTH API payloads; formulated concrete questions for live customer validation interviews.
Personas & Pain-Points
Generated first-pass list of operational logistics roles, daily responsibilities, and interview prompts. Pruned redundant stakeholder roles down to the two primary operational personas (Fleet Manager & Operations Director); restructured pain-points around actionable operational decisions rather than generic feelings.
Edge-Case Brainstorming
Produced an exhaustive raw list of failure scenarios across API synchronization, partial payments, ANPR OCR errors, and court summons. Filtered and synthesized into 16 mission-critical edge cases across 6 architectural areas; engineered the exact deterministic UI/UX pattern response for each.
Data & Copy for Screens
Generated realistic Indian vehicle registration series (MH12, DL01, KA03), legal citation codes, telemetry radar specs, and interface microcopy. Strictly aligned copy tone to enterprise ERP clarity; enforced mathematical coherence across financial ledgers, penalty totals, and vehicle counts across every screen.
Layout Architecture
Proposed alternative dashboard layout wireframes including high-density analytical dashboards and metric heatmaps. Rejected vanity reporting charts in favor of an actionable, prioritized “Work Queue” dashboard optimized for immediate resolution on Monday mornings.

Guardrail 1: Human-in-the-Loop

AI recommendations suggest attribution, but never silently bind financial liability without explicit manager or lessee confirmation.

Guardrail 2: Transparent Clause Citations

Every attribution score outputs the exact contract paragraph, GPS telematics log, or shift roster entry that justified the score.

Guardrail 3: Data Privacy Masking

Driver phone numbers, Aadhaar records, and personal banking identifiers are redacted before reaching public LLM inference endpoints.

17. Key Decisions & Strategic Trade-Offs

Strategic Decision Alternatives Considered Why This Way (The Strategic Moat) Trade-Off Accepted
Responsibility as time-based data with a reason Single static “assigned to” field Leasing demands date-based legal liability matching contract periods and an immutable audit trail for legal dispute resolution. More complex data model and requires additional explanatory interface elements.
Separate payer from responsible party One monolithic owner for both Fleet companies pay authorities immediately to avoid impoundment and compounding late fees, then recover from driver payroll or lessee invoice later. Introduces dual-ledger accounting concepts that require onboarding education.
Status tabs as primary navigation in lists Dropdown filters only Fleet workflows are inherently state-driven. Persistent status tabs immediately reveal workload volume and bottleneck counts at a single glance. Horizontally constrained to 6-7 primary lifecycle states; secondary states placed in sub-filters.
Dashboard as work queue Analytics & vanity chart dashboard Users arrive Monday morning with immediate actions to perform. Highlighting unassigned challans and urgent deadlines directly drives operator throughput. Fewer historical trend graphs in V1; prioritized operational speed over quarterly reporting.
Suggest then confirm Fully automated silent assignment Assigning liability directly triggers payroll deductions or contract penalties. Financial impact mandates human verification to prevent wrongful deductions. Requires one extra click per challan (mitigated via bulk confirmation actions).
Show sync time and source everywhere Hide technical backend details Trust in government API data determines whether fleet managers dare to act. Displaying exact sync timestamps and Parivahan/VAHAN source codes eliminates uncertainty. Slightly denser data tables requiring compact monospace metadata styling.
