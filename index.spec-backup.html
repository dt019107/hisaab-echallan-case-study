01. The Problem & How We Solved It

Why commercial traffic fines turn into financial chaos — and how Hisaab automates every step

Commercial Fleets & Vehicle Leasing
Visual Storytelling • Ground Reality

The Highway Reality: A Ticket Attaches to a Plate, Not a Person

National Highway NH-48 Telematics Case
Stage 1: Transit

4 Rotating Drivers Per Route

Commercial trucks operate 24/7. In a 48-hour run from Mumbai to Delhi, four different shift drivers swap seats at highway toll plazas.

Stage 2: Violation

ANPR Radar Traps Only Clock Plates

Police speed cameras record MH12 QR 2284 clocked at 84 km/h. The automated state portal notifies the registered company, not the driver.

Stage 3: Legal Fracture

Spreadsheet Limbo & Penalties

While fleet ops and drivers argue over who was driving, the 14-day court clock expires. Late fees double and commercial RTO permits get suspended.

The Real Problem in Fleets Today

Operational Chaos
  • Police cameras fine the truck, not the driver: When a truck runs a red light or speeds on a highway, the fine is tagged strictly to the vehicle registration number on the government portal (Parivahan). The system does not know who was driving.
  • Drivers change shifts every day: Delivery companies rotate 2 to 3 drivers per vehicle across different shifts. When a ticket arrives 3 weeks later, managers have no easy way to know who had the keys at that exact hour.
  • Lost deadlines mean double fines and locked trucks: Fleet teams track tickets on messy Excel sheets and WhatsApp groups. By the time someone notices, the 15-day court deadline has expired, late fees have doubled, and the government blocks the vehicle from renewing its road permit.

How Hisaab Fixes It (The UX Response)

Engineered Workflow
  • Action-first design, not passive reports: Every screen immediately answers two questions: “Who caused this fine?” and “What is the exact next step?”
  • GPS matching connects fine to driver in seconds: The platform reads the exact minute of the violation and cross-checks the vehicle's GPS trip log and driver roster, instantly showing who was at the wheel with a 98.4% confidence score.
  • Separated Payer vs Responsible Party: The fleet company pays the fine immediately from escrow so the truck stays legal on the road, while the system automatically queues a payroll deduction for the driver or adds it to the rental client's monthly bill.
  • AI helps the user, but humans make the final call: The system automates tedious data entry and rule-matching, but every fine attribution requires a simple one-click human confirmation before money moves.

7 Key Enterprise Stakeholders

In India’s commercial logistics industry, traffic fines affect two primary decision-makers: the Fleet Manager (who runs trucks and manages drivers) and the Lease Operations Lead (who rents vehicles to business clients). Five supporting roles handle payroll deduction, legal court dates, and driver disputes.

“We lease hundreds of commercial vans to logistics clients like Delhivery. If a client’s driver runs a red light, we must settle the fine immediately so the government doesn’t blacklist our vehicle, then bill the fine back to the client’s monthly invoice with proof.”

Top Priority: Immediate fine clearance & indisputable lease contract audit proof.

03. What the User Actually Needs: The 6 Decision Questions

Core Product Principle: “A traffic fine is not a record to be filed away — it is a decision to be made.”

Jobs-to-be-Done Framework

Jobs to be Done

What commercial operators hire Hisaab to achieve

  • Action on arrival: When challans arrive, tell me which need action today so I avoid compounding late fees.
  • Defensible attribution: When a challan appears, tell me who is responsible and why so I can defend the decision.
  • Bulk control at scale: When many vehicles are affected, let me act in bulk without losing individual governance.
  • Proof & clean ledger: When I pay, give me official government proof and keep the accounting ledger reconciled.
  • Audit-trailed changes: When responsibility is wrong or disputed, let me change it with an immutable audit trail.

Pain Points We Are Solving

Root operational friction points across commercial fleets

  • Visibility: Challans scattered across disconnected state police and Parivahan portals.
  • Attribution: The commercial vehicle is known, but the human driver on shift is not.
  • Timing: Statutory due dates and 15-day dispute windows missed, leading to double fines.
  • Data Quality: Duplicate or wrong registration numbers, stale ownership records, and failed syncs.
  • Recovery: Money paid by the fleet company to avoid permit freeze, but never recovered from driver or lessee.
  • Trust: Zero audit trail of who made decisions, why rules triggered, or when liability transferred.

07. Core Fleet Objects, Relational Schema & Global IA

To transition ChallanOS from an ad-hoc tracking spreadsheet to an enterprise-grade ERP system, we formalized the domain into 6 core relational entities and established 6 non-negotiable cross-cutting UX principles that govern every table, drawer, and modal.

The 6 Core Relational Fleet Objects

Strict 3NF Schema & Audit Trails
1 : N Entity

Vehicle

Holds the physical commercial asset profile. Has many Challans and many Responsibility Periods across historical fleet cycles.

Keys: reg_number (PK), vin, chassis
Relations: → Challans, → Contracts
Core Object

Challan

A legal citation. Has exactly one Vehicle, one Offence, one Status, one Responsible Party (with explicit business reason), and many Events.

Keys: challan_number (PK), notice_id
Relations: → Vehicle, → Party, → Events
Role Actor

Party

The human or legal actor. Represents Driver (on-duty employee/contractor), Lessee (commercial client), Fleet Operator, or Registered Owner.

Keys: party_id (PK), dl_number, gstin
Relations: → Custody_Periods, → Invoices
Legal Binding

Contract

The commercial leasing agreement. Links Owner, Lessee, Vehicle, effective timestamp windows, and statutory traffic liability clauses (e.g. Clause 7.2).

Keys: lease_id (PK), agreement_ref
Relations: → Vehicles, → Terms_Clause
Gateway Flow

Payment

The financial settlement event. Links Challan, Payer Entity, exact settlement amount, MoRTH gateway reference, receipt PDF, and secondary Recovery Ledger.

Keys: txn_id (PK), morth_ack_ref
Relations: → Challan, → Payroll_Deduction
Judicial Evidence

Dispute

Legal grievance or Virtual Court case. Links Challan, telemetry & ANPR evidence dossiers, statutory appeal deadlines, and court judgment outcome (Waived or Upheld).

Keys: dispute_id (PK), court_case_no
Relations: → Challan, → Evidence_Files

6 Cross-Cutting UX Principles

Universal Product Design Heuristics

1. Status First

One shared status vocabulary and uniform color token mapping used everywhere across cards, table pills, filters, and reports. No conflicting labels.

2. Explain the Why

Every attribution or AI recommendation explicitly renders the deterministic rule, telematics timestamp, or lease contract clause that produced it.

3. Money is Always Visible

Always formatted in Indian Rupee currency standard with tabular figures (number-tabular) and strict right-alignment.

4. Bulk by Default, Detail on Demand

Every data table is equipped with checkbox multi-select and a sticky floating action bar for bulk operations; slide-over drawer reveals deep item context.

5. Never Hide Data Quality

API synchronization timestamp, Parivahan gateway latency, and sync confidence score are treated as first-class UI citizens to preserve user trust.

6. Reversible Actions

Reassignment and dispute appeal pathways are always readily accessible with full cryptographic audit logging and versioned state transitions.

6. Figma File Structure, Variables & Auto-Layout Governance

Production Engineering Handoff Spec

Figma File Structure

Organized for atomic scalability across enterprise design teams

  • • Cover and changelog: Version tracking, design review approvals, token updates
  • • 00 Foundations: Colour, type, spacing, radius, icons (Figma Variables + Styles)
  • • 01 Components: Each with interactive variants, boolean properties, and states
  • • 02 Patterns: Filter bar, table with bulk bar, key-value list, responsibility panel, timeline
  • • 03 Screens: Six core deliverable screens plus deterministic edge-case frames
  • • 04 Prototype: End-to-end interactive decision flow from dashboard to payment receipt
  • • 05 Archive: Deprecated explorations and historical iterations
Figma Variables System
• Primitive collection: blue/50-900, gray/50-900, red, amber, green, violet, sky
• Semantic collection: color/text/primary, color/surface/default, color/border/default, color/action/primary, color/status/unassigned/bg and /fg
• Modes: Light now; Dark mode ready by remapping semantic tokens
• Number variables: space/4 to space/32, radius/sm to lg
• Components: Reference semantic variables exclusively, never hardcoded primitives or hex

Auto Layout Rules & Naming

Zero absolute positioning; predictable fluid resizing

  • 1. Auto-layout everywhere: Every component is built with auto layout; padding and gap come directly from space variables.
  • 2. Fixed columns with fill text: Table rows use fixed-width columns with fill-container for the main text column, so text wrap does not break grid alignment.
  • 3. Vertical hug & horizontal fill: Panels hug content vertically and fill horizontally.
  • 4. Dynamic button widths: Buttons and pills hug width with min width, so labels can change and be translated without clipping.
  • 5. Grid layout: Screens use a 12-column layout grid, 24px gutters, 240px collapsible sidebar.
  • 6. Component naming: Pattern is Category / Name (e.g. Status / Pill). Property names are consistent: Status, Size, State.
  • 7. Governance rule: Any new screen that needs a new style must first add a token or component.
Styling vs System: The status colours are the clearest example. They are not “red and blue boxes” hardcoded into each screen. They are one semantic token set (unassigned, assigned, pending, paid, disputed, court) consumed by the pill, dashboard bars, timeline dots, and receipt cards. Change one token and every screen updates instantly.

17. Key Decisions & Strategic Trade-Offs

High-stakes commercial compromises, defensible rationale, and quantifiable UX impact

3 Story Arcs • 7 Trade-Offs • 5 KPIs
Portfolio Deep-Dive • The 6-Part Narrative Arc

3 Defining Strategic Decisions

Title • Hook • Before • Conflict • Decision • Result

Hiring managers don't evaluate textbook process diagrams — they evaluate whether you can navigate messy organizational friction, make defensible trade-offs, and defend hard design compromises. Here are the three critical architectural decisions that defined Hisaab:

01

The Separation of Payer and Culprit (Dual-Ledger Treasury)

Strategic Business Model & Escrow Architecture
Strategic Tier
1. The Hook

If a fleet company waits for a commercial driver to acknowledge a ₹2,000 speeding fine, statutory Parivahan rules double the fine to ₹4,000 in 14 days and block vehicle fitness renewal.

2. The Before State

Fleet managers refused to pay out of fleet cash; drivers claimed they were off-duty. Tickets languished in spreadsheets for 45+ days, causing ₹18L/quarter in compounding penalties and highway truck detentions.

3. The Stakeholder Conflict

Finance Head: “Drivers must pay their own fines directly from UPI before we touch company money.”
Ops Director: “If we wait, RTO impounds our trucks on the highway. We must clear it immediately.”

4. The Design Decision

Decoupled the government settlement from internal accountability into a Dual-Ledger Escrow System. The fleet escrow settles with MoRTH within 24h; Hisaab auto-generates an internal payroll deduction docket with photographic proof.

5. The After Experience

Fleet manager clicks “Pay & Recover”. The government receives instant payment; the driver gets a WhatsApp notification containing the ANPR radar photo, location map, and a 7-day internal dispute appeal link.

6. The Measurable Result

• 92% on-time settlement before statutory deadlines
• 0 highway roadside truck detentions
• 96.5% payroll capital recovery on cycle

02

Time-Based Custody Windows vs. Static Driver Field

Data Architecture & Legal Defensibility
Architecture Tier
1. The Hook

A commercial inter-city truck has one license plate, but four rotating drivers in a single 48-hour haul across three state borders.

2. The Before State

Existing fleet tools had a static single input field: Assigned Driver: Ramesh Kumar. When a radar camera fine arrived, Ramesh was blamed even if Suresh was driving when the violation occurred 12 hours earlier.

3. The Stakeholder Conflict

Engineering Lead: “A 1:1 foreign key is clean and standard. Supporting multiple custody windows per day complicates the schema.”
Fleet Operations: “Driver unions threaten wildcat strikes if we dock pay from the wrong shift driver.”

4. The Design Decision

Re-architected the vehicle core data model into temporal custody windows (start_time, end_time, driver_id). The UI plots a chronological timeline bar showing red violation dots exactly where tickets intersect shift logs.

5. The After Experience

When viewing vehicle details (Screen 3), operators don't see an ambiguous name. They see a visual shift ribbon: at 14:22 on Pune Expressway, Driver Suresh held legal custody. Attribution is instant and undeniable.

6. The Measurable Result

• 100% defensible attribution rate
• 0 wrongful payroll deductions
• Driver attribution disputes dropped from 28% to < 3.5%

03

“Suggest, Explain, Confirm” vs. Blind AI Automation

Interaction Design & Heuristic Explainability
Interaction Tier
1. The Hook

Enterprise fleet directors will immediately kill and uninstall an automated compliance tool the first time an algorithm wrongfully penalizes an innocent client or driver without a human paper trail.

2. The Before State

Early design prototype explored “autonomous attribution” — the system auto-assigned tickets with >70% confidence and auto-billed the lessee. In early customer feedback, every single enterprise client vetoed the feature.

3. The Stakeholder Conflict

Product Management: “Automating 100% of assignments is our core marketing value prop.”
Corporate Legal Counsel: “Indian Motor Vehicles Act requires human responsibility attribution. Algorithms have no legal standing.”

4. The Design Decision

Replaced blind automation with the “Suggest, Explain, Confirm” heuristic model. The UI presents the AI recommendation, cites the exact contract clause (e.g., Clause 7.2 Driver Negligence), shows camera proof, and requires an explicit 1-click human confirmation.

5. The After Experience

On Screen 5, the manager sees: Suggested: Driver Suresh (94% confidence) along with the reasoning: “Shift logged 14:00-22:00 + Speeding violation at 17:34”. The manager clicks “Confirm & Notify” in under 2 seconds.

6. The Measurable Result

• 8.4x faster triage than manual spreadsheet checking
• 100% human accountability for all wage and lease billing
• Zero legal disputes regarding algorithmic bias
